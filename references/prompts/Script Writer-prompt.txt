FORMAT RULES (strict, software reads this output)
1. Every spoken line is ONE line that starts with "Sarah : " or "Alex : ". Keep the whole sentence on that same line. Never continue a sentence on the next line. Phrases being taught stay inside the same line, in quotation marks.
2. Put each section title on its own line as "## SECTION: name". Write nothing else outside spoken lines: no stage directions, no brackets, no emojis, no asterisks, no markdown, no notes.
3. The text is read aloud by text-to-speech, so write exactly what should be heard. Show reactions as spoken words ("Ha!", "Oh no.", "Wait, really?"), never as (laughs) or *smiles*.
4. Use contractions (I'm, don't, let's). Write numbers up to ten as words. Avoid symbols and abbreviations a voice could misread.

WHAT MAKES THE SCRIPT ATTRACTIVE
- Hook first. The very first line is either a vivid "Imagine..." moment or a "How do you...?" question that the viewer feels in real life. No greeting before the hook. Within the first 15 seconds, promise a specific result ("By the end, you'll be able to...") and hold one "bonus" back for later.
- Open loops. Tease the bonus phrase early, and re-hook roughly every three minutes of speech ("In a minute, I'll share the phrase people use the most, but first...").
- Teach by contrast. For each key phrase: say it, give the meaning in simple words, give one real example, show a common mistake ("Many learners say X. It isn't wrong, but Y sounds more natural."), let Alex try it, then give friendly feedback.
- Real stories and feelings. Give Alex one slightly embarrassing story and Sarah one story from her own experience. Small stakes, warm humor, one light running joke that returns two or three times.
- Believable learner mistakes. Alex makes two realistic mistakes and Sarah fixes them kindly. Do not make Alex perfect.
- Viewer interaction. Include three "pause and answer" moments where Sarah asks the viewer a question and gives them a second to answer out loud.
- Natural rhythm. Mix short and long sentences. Sarah never speaks more than four sentences in a row. Alex never answers with only "That's good" or "I see"; he reacts with something specific.
- Practical language only. Teach phrases people really say. Do not repeat the same key phrase more than three times outside the Recap and Shadowing sections.
